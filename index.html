02 / 06 — The route

Five legs, one long exhale.

  1. 01

    Ascent Day 0–3

    Launch from Port Meridian, dock with the cruiser in a 400 km parking orbit, and spend three days watching a sunrise every ninety minutes.

  2. 02

    Lunar slingshot Day 4

    A close pass behind the Moon bends our path and saves a week of fuel. Most guests see the far side here for the first time.

  3. 03

    Trans-Mars injection Day 5

    Six minutes of engine burn. After that the ship falls toward Mars on a curve calculated two years in advance.

  4. 04

    The coast Day 6–208

    Two hundred days of quiet. Earth shrinks to a blue star; Mars grows from a spark to a coin.

  5. 05

    Aerocapture Day 210

    The ship skims the Martian upper atmosphere to brake for free, then settles into orbit.

03 / 06 — The ship

The Meridian-class cruiser.

Built for long nights, not short thrills. Every system has a twin, and every cabin has a window.

  • 0.38 g

    Spun gravity

    A 64-metre ring turns four times a minute for Mars-normal gravity, so you arrive walking.

  • 6 m

    Panorama deck

    A fused-silica dome faces the direction of travel. It is the quietest room in the solar system.

  • 12 / 12

    Storm shelter

    A water-walled core protects every guest during solar storms. Drills are weekly; naps are optional.

  • 9 m²

    Private cabins

    A bunk that folds into a desk, a soft amber reading light and your own porthole.

04 / 06 — Life aboard

A day on the coast.

  1. 07:00

    Ring sunrise

    Cabin lights warm slowly over twenty minutes.

  2. 08:30

    Training

    Simulated hull walks, medical basics and Mars surface protocol.

  3. 12:00

    Long table

    Crew and guests eat together, mostly from the hydroponic bay.

  4. 15:00

    Free hours

    Library, observatory, letters home — up to 22 minutes each way.

  5. 21:00

    Dome time

    The lights go out and the stars come on.

05 / 06 — Arrival · Day 210

Mars, up close.

  • Olympus Mons at dawn

    Three times the height of Everest, seen edge-on as the terminator crosses it.

  • Valles Marineris

    A canyon as long as a continent, traced from orbit on a slow polar pass.

  • Phobos rendezvous

    Float alongside a moon nine kilometres wide that is slowly falling toward its planet.

  • Jezero landing

    Surface fare only: eleven days in a pressurised habitat on an ancient river delta.
